# Click synchronization investigation — 2026-10-05

This concerns the click/audio offset, separately from the earlier three-source repair. Evidence directories below are relative to the repository and ignored; they may not exist in another clone. Scripts and protocols are versioned for repeatable measurements. Missing local evidence is not permission to invent a historical result.

The final desktop music matrix passes, and measured click/music pairs align after the repair. ## Established cause and repair

The earlier 1,914-capture music audit used an empty beat grid and did not measure click output. The click scheduler predicted beat output from `HTMLMediaElement.currentTime`, `AudioContext.currentTime`, and the music processing delay. Actual PCM showed that the native media clock did not map reliably to the Web Audio sample timeline in the tested WebKit runtimes. A native clock could also temporarily stop advancing while the old lookahead continued predicting beats. This explains a playback-layer offset independently of the beat-tracking model.

`audio-audit.local/click-six-baseline-20261005/` and `click-six-candidate-20261005/` retain matched six-channel recordings, clock maps, native iOS evidence and raw PCM. The intermediate JS scheduler removed future scheduling during a frozen clock but retained approximately 65 ms desktop WebKit / 31 ms iOS click offsets. It was not accepted. Its patch/source remain in `click-js-scheduler-candidate-20261005/`, outside the active test tree.

The integrated repair carries normal/downbeat cues in two extra channels of the same native transport as the three music pairs. A separate AudioWorklet delays cue events by the music processor's queried latency, then synthesizes a fixed-tone click at output sample rate. Cue samples never enter music summing or pitch processing. Stop, seek and speed changes gate/reset both branches. Reanalysis changes a cue-content revision; the client rejects mismatched cached transport audio.

Eight-channel PCM WAV exceeds ordinary RIFF capacity before the existing two-hour limit. The first cue candidate therefore used lossless FLAC, but the long-fixture seek comparison below rejected it. The replacement uses PCM16/48 kHz WAV with RF64 when required by size, bounded generation and atomic cache publication. Original-only tracks with beats also receive cue transport, using silent stem lanes. Preparation/failure is visible, and next-track autoplay waits for the initial beat query and required transport preparation.

Final lifecycle review found that immediately installing prepared cue media would stop original-only playback, extending the previous late-separation behavior to ordinary tracks. Prepared and active media are now separate: playback, pending native Play, play restoration and end draining retain the active source; Stop installs the prepared source at the preserved cursor without restarting. Cue availability follows the active transport's match to the verified current grid, and separated controls follow the active transport's kind. Manual button/keyboard Play or Stop also consumes a pending next-track automatic request, so later readiness cannot override the user's Stop or toggle an already-playing original. Focused tests preserve dirty markers, speed, transpose and levels across this transition.

Three separate production-graph captures in `click-cue-end-replay-20261005/` showed that explicit Replay after native/UI end readiness works, and final output becomes silent after the measured 120 ms processing tail. These candidate results do not yet establish a release-ready transport; container/seek-metadata comparisons are required before rerunning the affected matrix.

The controlled comparison in `click-cue-container-seek-20261005/` preserves four files whose decoded PCM SHA256 hashes are identical. Baseline FLAC and FLAC with an added SEEKTABLE both begin at 238.056 s after seeking to 239.4 s. RIFF WAV and RF64 WAV both begin exactly at 239.4 s, play the remaining 0.6 s and retain a 240 s duration. The WAV repair preserves the existing sample rate and precision. The cache remains bounded by the two-hour limit and is invalidated by source/grid content; the rejected FLAC cache is not reused.

## Two-hour RF64 qualification

The fixture has source identities in its first second and final eight seconds, a silent middle, and final cue pulses. The experiment warms the native element, reloads, seeks to 7198.4 seconds, then records through the 7200-second end. It does not run the production React/pitch/click graph or a two-hour continuous decoding workload.

`rf64-check-1791183148101218000.json` records **two passes and one failure**. Both desktop engines reproduce the full requested 76,800-frame segment on every lane with zero sample error, the correct end state, and actual byte ranges beyond 4 GiB. Chromium converts positive PCM16 values by multiplying by float32(1/32767), negative values by float32(1/32768); WebKit uses 1/32768 for both signs. The original single-model failure is preserved. The checker now identifies either exact arithmetic model without a tolerance or fitted gain; 16 calibration cases reject one-sample displacement, music/cue corruption, truncated endings, wrong gain and swapped channels.

The iOS recording also reads beyond 4 GiB and reproduces the complete requested segment exactly, but first repeats its initial 128 samples (2.667 ms). `rf64-native-repeated-prefix-diagnosis.json` preserves exact eight-channel comparisons and event timing: the repeated prefix is after native Play, not leftover audio from the earlier warmup position. The strict continuity result remains failed. The initial fitter's apparent −128-frame start was an extrapolation across this discontinuity, not an actual early source position. This isolated native input finding does not establish audible behavior through production output gates. It demonstrates large-offset access and retained payload identity, but does not justify claiming flawless native source continuity.

## Remaining native boundary defect

Relative click alignment and source completeness are separate checks. Native WebKit can omit audio at fresh start or seek; the new click test retains this as failure. This was reproduced with the previous six-channel path and isolated native media, so it is not established as a FLAC/cue regression. Do not label the overall click suite green while it remains.

`click-native-mechanics-20261005/` compared padding, warm seeking and reload. Padding protected file start; warm seeking could emit stale PCM; reload removed stale timelines but retained variable leading loss. A fixed 85 ms correction is not a demonstrated general solution.

`click-native-start-order-20261005/` compared immediate seek/play with waiting for readiness. All eight desktop captures completed. Chromium preserved requested starts. In WebKit, immediate fresh epsilon play began around 80.0208 ms; reload to 1 s began at 1.0800208 s immediately or 1.0960208 s after waiting. Fresh epsilon plus waiting preserved sample zero in that recording, but did not solve reload/seek. Seven synthetic checks verify missing blocks, old/pre-target PCM and invalid action ordering. These experiments do not modify production transport.

The investigation inspected [WebKit's native source provider](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/avfoundation/AudioSourceProviderAVFObjC.mm) and [bug 288879](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=288879) as related leads. Current upstream source and a previously fixed stale-audio report do not prove the identical defect in the installed Safari build.
